MetaLoom itself is open source under the **Apache License 2.0**. What you may do with a *running*
MetaLoom deployment, however, is not decided by the platform license alone: the pipeline nodes call
out to third-party **model weights**, and those weights carry their own licenses. A handful of them
forbid commercial use outright.

This section collects everything you need to answer two questions before you deploy:

* *Which models does my pipeline actually pull in, and what am I allowed to do with them?*
* *How was the MetaLoom source code produced, and where was AI code generation involved?*

== The Platform License

The MetaLoom source — the Loom server, the Cortex engine, the nodes, the UI, the clients and this
website — is licensed under the **Apache License, Version 2.0**. The full text ships as
`LICENSE.txt` in the repository root.

Apache-2.0 permits commercial use, modification, distribution and private use, subject to the usual
attribution and NOTICE obligations. Nothing on this page restricts that.

[IMPORTANT]
====
**The platform license does not travel to the model weights.** MetaLoom ships *no* model weights.
Nodes reference models by path, repository id or endpoint URL, and you supply them. The license that
governs a model is the one on that model's card — not Apache-2.0.
====

== Read This First If You Are Going Commercial

Two things in the catalogue are hard blockers for a commercial deployment:

* the **InspireFace model packs** used by the link:../nodes/facedetect/[Face Detection] node, and
* **Ideogram 4.0**, the backing model of the planned `imagegen` node.

Both are covered in detail under
link:model-licenses/#restricted[Models restricted to non-commercial use], together with the swaps and
workarounds that exist. Everything else in the default catalogue is either permissively licensed or
carries conditions you can meet.

== Not Legal Advice

These pages are an engineering inventory maintained alongside the code, not a legal opinion. Model
licenses change between releases, and a checkpoint's terms can differ from the terms of the
architecture it is based on. Before you ship, verify the license on the model card of the *exact*
revision you deploy, and involve your own counsel where the answer matters.
